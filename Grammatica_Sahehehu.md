# Grammatica Sãhëħëħu
## A Grammar of Sãhëħëħu (Shanghainese)

> **Status:** Descriptive grammar based on the current Sãhëħëħu lexicon and example sentences.  
> **Note:** Rules not yet sufficiently supported by examples are marked as provisional.

---

## 0. Introduction

**Sãhëħëħu** is an analytic language whose grammar relies primarily on word order, particles, auxiliary-like words, and grammaticalized verbal particles rather than inflection.

The basic constituent order is:

> **Subject – Verb – Object (SVO)**

Major grammatical features include:

- a productive copula **z / s** “to be”
- an indefinite article **î'e**
- progressive aspect **lehë**
- perfective marking **re / le**
- experiential marking **gu**
- resultative **tê**
- causative/change-of-state **nõ**
- existential/possessive **io**
- negative verbal construction **ve iɔ + V**
- sentence-final interrogative **va**
- productive serial-verb constructions

---

# I. Orthography

Sãhëħëħu is written in a Latin-based orthography containing modified Latin letters and phonetic symbols.

Examples:

> ŋ, ɕ, ʑ, ø, ə, ã, ë, ɔ, ʉ, ħ

The apostrophe **'** is also grammatically significant in certain forms.

Examples:

> î'e  
> ara'e  
> gɔɕɔ'e

---

# II. Basic Word Order

The default word order is:

> **Subject + Verb + Object**

Examples:

> **Ńu bã i.**  
> I help him/her.

> **Ńu cɕi Yãgã.**  
> I go to Hong Kong.

> **I bã ŋu.**  
> He/she helps me.

The subject normally precedes the predicate, while the direct object follows the verb.

---

# III. Personal Pronouns

Sãhëħëħu does not distinguish grammatical gender in the third-person singular pronoun.

| Pronoun | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ŋu | I / me |
| na | you |
| i | he / she / it |
| ara | we / us |
| ira | they / them |

Pronouns generally do not change form according to grammatical case.

Examples:

> **Ńu bã i.**  
> I help him/her.

> **I bã ŋu.**  
> He/she helps me.

---

# IV. The Copula

## 4.1 z / s — “to be”

The copula is:

> **z / s**

It corresponds to English **be**.

The basic structure is:

> **Subject + z/s + Predicate**

### Nominal predication

> **ŋu z î'e nĩ.**  
> I am a person.

Morphological analysis:

> `ŋu | z | î'e | nĩ`  
> `I | COP | a | person`

Thus:

> **z / s = COPULA “be”**

The two forms **z** and **s** are currently treated as forms of the same copula unless further corpus evidence establishes a distinction.

---

# V. The Indefinite Article

## 5.1 î'e

**î'e** functions as an indefinite article before a singular count noun.

It corresponds approximately to:

> **a / an**

Structure:

> **î'e + Noun**

Examples:

> **î'e nĩ**  
> a person

> **î'e mez**  
> a thing

Therefore:

> **ŋu z î'e nĩ.**  
> I am a person.

The structure is:

> `ŋu | z | î'e | nĩ`  
> `I | be | a | person`

---

# VI. Nouns and Noun Phrases

A basic noun phrase may contain:

> **Determiner + Modifier + Noun**

Examples:

> **î'e nĩ**  
> a person

> **ge mez**  
> this thing

> **ge-czõ mez**  
> this kind of thing

The exact ordering of all nominal modifiers remains subject to further corpus analysis.

---

# VII. Demonstratives

The basic demonstrative is:

> **ge** = this

Examples:

> **Ge mez.**  
> This thing.

> **Ge difã.**  
> This place.

A more specific demonstrative construction is:

> **ge-czõ** = this kind of

Example:

> **ge-czõ mez**  
> this kind of thing

---

# VIII. Adjectives

Adjectives can function directly as predicates.

Examples:

> **Ńu czbugø.**  
> I am thirsty.

> **Ge ticɕi lɔ nië'e.**  
> The weather is very hot.

---

## 8.1 The adjective marker -e

The suffix:

> **-'e**

can explicitly mark an adjective.

Example:

> `gɔɕɔ → gɔɕɔ'e`

> funny → adjectival funny

Example sentence:

> **Ge mez lɔ gɔɕɔ'e.**  
> This thing is very funny.

The marker is not necessarily obligatory in every environment.

---

# IX. Degree Modification

## 9.1 lɔ

The degree modifier:

> **lɔ**

means approximately:

> very / highly / quite

Structure:

> **lɔ + adjective**

Examples:

> **lɔ nië'e**  
> very hot

> **lɔ gɔɕɔ'e**  
> very funny

> **lɔ czõħiɔ**  
> very important

Other degree expressions include:

| Form | Meaning |
|---|---|
| îdi | a little |
| tɕio | a little / just |
| gaɕidu | extremely many |
| ʑacɕi | extremely / very much |
| gorê | enough |
| tɕĩnʉ | rather |

---

# X. Verbs

Sãhëħëħu verbs generally do not inflect for person or number.

The same verbal form may occur with different subjects:

> **Ńu cɕi.**  
> I go.

> **na cɕi.**  
> You go.

> **i cɕi.**  
> He/she goes.

Grammatical information is primarily expressed through independent particles and auxiliary-like words.

---

# XI. Progressive Aspect

## 11.1 lehë

**lehë** marks an action that is in progress.

Structure:

> **Subject + lehë + Verb**

Example:

> **Ńu lehë bã i bêɕã.**  
> I am playing with him/her.

> **Ira lehë dãɕiãdã.**  
> They are fighting.

Thus:

> **lehë + V**  
> ≈ *be V-ing*

---

# XII. Perfective Aspect

## 12.1 re / le

**re / le** marks a completed event.

It commonly occurs at the end of the predicate or sentence.

### Direct attachment

> **V + re**

Example:

> **Ńu czuhɔre.**  
> I finished.

### Independent final particle

> **Ńu da gørʑin le.**  
> I washed it clean.

> **Ńu nõtê i le.**  
> I removed it.

The exact distribution of **re** and **le** requires further corpus verification.

---

# XIII. Experiential Aspect

## 13.1 gu

**gu** marks an event experienced at least once.

Structure:

> **V + gu**

Example:

> **Ńu cɕigu Yãgã.**  
> I have been to Hong Kong.

Question:

> **Nõ kødɔgu ge-czõ mez va?**  
> Have you ever seen this kind of thing?

The distinction is:

> **V + re/le** = completed event

> **V + gu** = past experience / “have ever V-ed”

Thus **gu** is best analyzed as an experiential aspect marker rather than simply a past-tense marker.

---

# XIV. Resultative Particle

## 14.1 tê

**tê** functions as a resultative particle.

It expresses a resulting state such as:

- removal
- disappearance
- completion
- termination

Structure:

> **V + tê**

Examples:

> **nõtê**  
> remove / dispose of completely

> **ɕitê**  
> die

The construction is therefore:

> **V + RESULTATIVE**

rather than merely a tense marker.

---

# XV. Causative / Change-of-State Verb

## 15.1 nõ

**nõ** expresses:

> make  
> cause  
> cause to become

Structure:

> **nõ + Predicate**

Examples:

> **Nõ gørʑin.**  
> Make it clean.

> **Nõ tê i.**  
> Get rid of it.

Thus **nõ** introduces a caused state or action.

---

# XVI. Modal Constructions

## 16.1 Need / obligation

**iɔ** can express necessity or obligation.

Structure:

> **iɔ + Verb**

Example:

> **Nõ iɔ ɕioɕî.**  
> You need to rest.

---

## 16.2 Ability / permission

**hɔ** expresses ability or permission.

Structure:

> **hɔ + Verb**

Examples:

> **Gede hɔ zu va?**  
> Can one sit here?

> **Nõ hɔ bãmã va?**  
> Can you help?

---

# XVII. Verbal Negation

The basic negative verbal construction is:

> **ve iɔ + Verb**

This is treated as a grammatical construction rather than translating **ve** alone as “not”.

Structure:

> **ve iɔ + V**

Examples:

> **ve iɔ cɕi**  
> do not go

> **ve iɔ bɔ**  
> do not run

Thus:

> **ve = negative element**

> **iɔ = verbal/modal element**

The exact historical relationship between **ve** and **iɔ** remains open for further analysis.

---

# XVIII. Prohibitive

A separate prohibitive construction is:

> **viɔ + Verb**

Examples:

> **Viɔ dã i!**  
> Don't hit him/her!

> **Viɔ cɔɕiãmu le!**  
> Don't quarrel anymore!

The distinction between:

> **ve iɔ + V**

and:

> **viɔ + V**

should be determined from additional examples.

---

# XIX. Imperatives

The simple imperative can consist of the bare verb:

> **Rë!**  
> Come!

> **Kø!**  
> Look!

> **Bê!**  
> Give!

Negative commands use the negative/prohibitive constructions described above.

---

# XX. Serial Verb Constructions

Sãhëħëħu permits two or more verbs to occur consecutively.

Structure:

> **V₁ + V₂**

This may express:

- sequential actions
- motion + purpose
- coordinated actions

For example:

> **Cɕi + V**

may mean:

> go and V / go to V

Sãhëħëħu therefore has a productive **serial verb construction (SVC)** system.

---

# XXI. Existential and Possessive Construction

## 21.1 io

**io** has an existential function.

Structure:

> **Location + io + NP**

Example:

> **Ëmidê io czê mɔ.**  
> There is a cat there.

---

## 21.2 Possession

The same construction can express possession:

> **Subject + io + NP**

Meaning:

> S has X.

Thus the language appears to use an existential construction as a principal strategy for possession.

---

# XXII. Locative Expressions

Important spatial expressions include:

| Form | Meaning |
|---|---|
| gede | here |
| ëmidê | there |
| aridê | where |
| liɕã | inside |
| ŋado | outside |
| gɔdo | above / on top |
| odo | behind |
| ztê | beside |
| cztɕi | between |
| iŋa | beyond |
| bãbi | around / near |

Spatial relations are frequently expressed through lexical particles and serial constructions.

---

# XXIII. Questions

Sãhëħëħu does not require English-style subject–auxiliary inversion.

The basic word order is normally retained.

---

## 23.1 Wh-questions

Question words occur in their normal syntactic positions.

Examples:

> **Na czu sa?**  
> What are you doing?

> **Nõ lehë aridê?**  
> Where are you?

Important interrogatives include:

| Form | Meaning |
|---|---|
| sa | what |
| ari | which |
| ari-czõ | which kind |
| ari-bi | which side |
| aridê | where |

---

## 23.2 Yes/No questions

**va** is a sentence-final interrogative particle.

Structure:

> **Clause + va?**

Examples:

> **Nõ hɔ bãmã va?**  
> Can you help?

> **Nõ tɕigu lõ va?**  
> Have you seen a loong before?

---

# XXIV. Adverbs

Adverbs generally occur before the predicate or at the beginning of the sentence.

Examples:

> **ħîtɕido** = suddenly

> **bîdĩ** = certainly

> **muzã** = soon

> **ʑido** = ago

> **îgado** = alone

> **izë** = now

> **zʉħo** = then

> **kunẽ** = perhaps

---

# XXV. Conjunction and Coordination

The language contains several conjunction-like particles.

**a** can express “also” in a parallel proposition.

Example:

> **Tɕia lɔ iocɔpiɔ'e, Dĩ a lɔ iocɔpiɔ'e.**  
> Tɕia is very rich, and Dĩ is also very rich.

The multifunctional form **bã** may express relations including “and” and “with”, depending on construction.

---

# XXVI. Benefactive Construction

A benefactive construction is:

> **bã + Person + ma + Object**

with the meaning:

> buy something for somebody

Example:

> **Ńu bã i ma're ge.**  
> I bought him/her this.

This may be analyzed as a serial/juxtaposed verbal construction.

---

# XXVII. Reduplication

Reduplication is productive in Sãhëħëħu.

Examples:

> **bã-bã**

> **kø-kø**

Depending on the lexical item and context, reduplication may express:

- repetition
- emphasis
- intensity
- plurality
- lexicalization

The full semantic range requires a larger corpus.

---

# XXVIII. Numerals

Numerals generally precede the noun or counting unit.

Examples:

> **be-bĩ**  
> eight bottles

> **në kuëħãri**  
> 20 dollars

---

## 28.1 Age

**sø** expresses age in years.

Structure:

> **Number + sø**

Example:

> **I së-sø.**  
> He/she is three years old.

---

## 28.2 Ordinals and numbering

The prefix:

> **di-**

is used in numbered or ordinal expressions.

Example:

> **di-ni'e**  
> number two / second

---

# XXIX. Tense and Aspect

The present system is better described as **aspect-oriented** than as a simple past/present/future tense system.

| Form | Function |
|---|---|
| V | unmarked event |
| lehë + V | progressive |
| V + gu | experiential |
| V + re/le | perfective |
| V + tê | resultative |
| lɔczɔ + predicate | past/background context |

Future meaning may be expressed through context, temporal adverbs, or modal constructions. A dedicated future marker has not yet been established.

# XXXI. Grammatical Formula

```text
CLAUSE
    = SUBJECT + PREDICATE

PREDICATE
    = VERB + OBJECT
    | COPULA + NOUN PHRASE
    | ADJECTIVE
    | MODAL + VERB
    | EXISTENTIAL CONSTRUCTION

COPULA
    = z / s

NOMINAL PREDICATION
    = S + z/s + NP

INDEFINITE NP
    = î'e + N

PROGRESSIVE
    = S + lehë + V

PERFECTIVE
    = S + V + ... + re/le

EXPERIENTIAL
    = S + V-gu

RESULTATIVE
    = V-tê

CAUSATIVE
    = nõ + PREDICATE

MODAL
    = MODAL + V

NEGATIVE
    = ve iɔ + V

PROHIBITIVE
    = viɔ + V

EXISTENTIAL
    = PLACE + io + NP

POSSESSION
    = S + io + NP

QUESTION
    = CLAUSE + va
    | WH + CLAUSE

SERIAL VERB
    = V₁ + V₂

ADJECTIVE
    = ROOT + e
    (where explicit adjectival marking is required)

DEGREE
    = lɔ + ADJECTIVE
```

# XXXII. Typological Profile

Sãhëħëħu can provisionally be characterized as:

> **an analytic, predominantly SVO language with a grammatical copula, serial-verb constructions, strong aspectual marking, resultative constructions, and relatively little verbal inflection.**

Its principal grammatical characteristics are:

1. **SVO basic word order**
2. **z / s as the copula “to be”**
3. **î'e as an indefinite article**
4. **No person agreement on verbs**
5. **Progressive aspect with lehë**
6. **Perfective aspect with re/le**
7. **Experiential aspect with gu**
8. **Resultative tê**
9. **Causative/change-of-state nõ**
10. **Existential/possessive io**
11. **Negative verbal construction ve iɔ**
12. **Sentence-final interrogative va**
13. **Productive serial verbs**
14. **Direct adjectival predication**
15. **Productive reduplication**
16. **Extensive spatial vocabulary**

# XXXIII. Minimal Paradigm

> **ŋu z î'e nĩ.**  
> I am a person.

> **ŋu bã i.**  
> I help him/her.

> **ŋu lehë bã i.**  
> I am helping him/her.

> **ŋu bã i gu.**  
> I have experienced helping/meeting him/her before.

> **ŋu bã i le.**  
> I completed the action of helping him/her.

> **ve iɔ bã i.**  
> Do not help him/her.

> **ŋu iɔ ɕioɕî.**  
> I need to rest.

> **ŋu hɔ cɕi.**  
> I can go.

> **ŋu z czbugø.**  
> I am thirsty.

> **ŋu io î'e mez.**  
> I have a thing.

## XXXIV. Summary

The central structure of Sãhëħëħu can be reduced to a small number of highly productive grammatical mechanisms:

```text
S + V + O
S + z/s + NP
î'e + N
lehë + V
V + gu
V + re/le
V + tê
nõ + Predicate
ve iɔ + V
viɔ + V
PLACE + io + NP
S + io + NP
Clause + va
```

These constructions form the current core of **Grammatica Sãhëħëħu**.
